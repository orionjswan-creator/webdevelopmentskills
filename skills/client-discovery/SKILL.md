# Client Discovery

Premium engagements aren't priced by page count — they're priced by **business outcome,
craft depth, and risk absorbed**. Discovery is where that value is established: a thin brief
produces a template; a real discovery produces the inputs every downstream skill needs
(BRIEF.md → DIRECTION.md → ARCHITECTURE.md) and a scope document both sides can trust.

## The interview (run as a structured conversation, one topic at a time)

Adapt depth to the client; capture verbatim quotes — they become copy and rationale later.

**1. Business reality**
- What does the business actually sell, to whom, at what price point?
- Where does revenue come from today, and what should the site change about that?
- The 12-month goal this site serves (launch, reposition, more bookings, higher-value
  leads?) — push past "we need a new website" to the outcome behind it.

**2. Audience**
- Describe the last 3 customers won: how did they find you, what convinced them?
- What do prospects consistently misunderstand or ask about?
- Who influences the decision (the buyer's boss, the couple's planner, the CFO)?

**3. Positioning & taste**
- 3 competitors they envy and why; 3 they refuse to resemble and why (feeds
  competitive-research directly).
- 5 adjectives for how the brand should *feel*; 3 it must never feel (feeds design-taste).
- Sites/brands from ANY industry they admire (taste calibration, not templates to copy).

**4. Content & assets reality check** (the classic budget-killer — audit it now)
- What exists: photography? copy? brand guidelines? logo files (vector?)? video?
- Who writes/approves copy? Who owns photography? (If "we'll handle it" — flag as the #1
  schedule risk and offer the asset-creation + site-copywriter skills as scope.)

**5. Function & integration**
- Booking/commerce/CRM/newsletter/analytics systems in use — names and versions.
- Who edits the site after launch, how often, and how technical are they? (feeds
  cms-integration: this answer swings scope more than any other.)
- Compliance context: jurisdiction, sector regulations, accessibility obligations (public
  sector / EU market → accessibility-compliance skill becomes mandatory scope).

**6. Success & constraints**
- What number, 6 months post-launch, proves this worked? (bookings/week, demo requests,
  average order value…)
- Hard deadline and what drives it; budget range if they'll share it; decision maker and
  approval process (how many people must say yes to a design?).

## Deliverable 1 — `DISCOVERY.md`

Findings organized: business context · audience profiles (2–3, with the verbatim quotes) ·
positioning map (envied/refused competitors) · content inventory + gaps · systems list ·
success metrics · risks (named, with owners). End with a **recommendation block**: proposed
style skill, proposed scope tier, and the 2–3 discovery insights that should shape the
creative direction.

## Deliverable 2 — `BRIEF.md`

The site-builder Phase-0 template, filled from discovery — this is the machine-readable
handoff to the rest of the library.

## Deliverable 3 — `SOW.md` (the scope contract)

Structure a premium engagement in phases mapped to this library, each with deliverables,
review gates, and revision allowances:

| Phase | Deliverables | Library skills |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery & strategy | DISCOVERY.md, BRIEF.md, RESEARCH.md, ARCHITECTURE.md | this skill, competitive-research, ux-architecture |
| 2. Identity & direction | DIRECTION.md, brand kit (if scoped), 1–2 design concepts | brand-identity, design-taste |
| 3. Design & build | The site per the pipeline, asset manifest, copy deck | site-builder, asset-creation, site-copywriter, ui-patterns |
| 4. Quality & compliance | QA report, WCAG audit + statement (if scoped) | site-auditor, accessibility-compliance |
| 5. Launch & handoff | Deployed site, CMS + training, LAUNCH.md, HANDOFF package | launch-seo, cms-integration, client-delivery |
| 6. Care (optional retainer) | Updates, trend-watch, performance monitoring | competitive-research trend-watch, site-auditor |

**Scope discipline that protects both sides:**
- Enumerate: page templates (by name), unique components, content entries migrated,
  languages, integrations. Everything else is change-order territory.
- Revision policy: N structured feedback rounds per phase (2 is standard), consolidated
  comments from ONE decision maker; define what a "round" is in writing.
- Client dependencies with dates (content, photography, approvals) and the schedule
  consequence when they slip — say it kindly, in the SOW, not angrily, in month three.
- Assumptions section: hosting, licenses (fonts! stock! plugins), third-party costs
  carried by the client.
- Payment tied to phase gates, not calendar dates.

## What justifies the premium (write the proposal narrative around these)

1. **Strategy before pixels** — discovery, research, and IA as paid, delivered artifacts.
2. **Original identity-level design** — a direction defended with rationale (client-delivery
   skill), not three template options.
3. **Craft depth** — motion choreography, custom assets, performance and accessibility
   engineering documented against a published quality bar.
4. **Content as scope** — copywriting and art-directed assets included, not "client to
   provide" fine print that sinks the schedule.
5. **Operational completeness** — CMS the client can actually use, training, documentation,
   measurement, and a care path.

Present pricing as investment vs. the discovery metric ("if this lifts bookings 15%…") and
offer tiers by *depth* (scope of phases 2/4/6), never by "quality" — the quality bar is the
same at every tier; that's the brand promise.

## Rules

- Never proceed to build with an unfilled BRIEF.md — thin discovery is how 50K projects
  become 15K projects with 50K expectations.
- Don't promise awards; promise the quality bar and the process that wins them.
- If the client's real need is a landing page, say so and route to rapid-site-sprint at
  honest scope — trust converts to the bigger engagement later.
